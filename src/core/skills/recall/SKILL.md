# Recall

Recover the smallest useful current-state brief from authorized history and live evidence. Context recovery does not authorize implementation or publication.

1. **Scope before reading.** Pin topic, workspace and requested time window; state reasonable defaults when unspecified (active workspace and the last seven days for “recent”). “All” remains all: report access/coverage limits rather than silently narrowing it. Use a supplied complete capsule instead of mining. For one named session, target that session and its linked artifacts.
2. **Discover available records.** Use the host's history/index capabilities and actual project sources, without assuming transcript directories or a source-control provider. Search titles, metadata and topic terms first, then read bounded relevant passages in batches. Skip the current conversation, unrelated projects and child/eval noise; de-duplicate resumed or copied records. Keep raw histories private and immutable, returning minimal paraphrases and locators. If summaries disagree about actions, inspect the relevant original tool/action records.
3. **Reconcile the shared record.** Follow relevant linked changes, issues, decisions and incidents when they affect the named work. Expand only to resolve material status or rationale gaps; avoid a mandatory connector sweep. State what was consulted and what remains inaccessible.
4. **Refresh consequential claims.** Check current checkout/revision, dirty work, branch/change status and relevant runtime or deployment identity through available tools. Keep historical reports separate from current observations. A timeout, inaccessible source or stale test result leaves that claim unknown; a merge is not proof of deployment. Preserve unrelated work without resetting, rebasing or changing it.
5. **Return a brief.** Lead with a compact state capsule, then one line per relevant thread with an evidence-supported status, unresolved problems and one concrete next move. Include source locators, freshness and coverage gaps. Use statuses such as planned, in progress, validated at a named revision, open, merged, reverted or unknown as the evidence permits; avoid forcing uncertain work into a success label.
