# Lead with the answer

Put the answer or decision first and the background after it, so a reader who stops after one sentence still knows the outcome.

1. Before writing, state the answer in one sentence: the decision, result, verdict or current state. If the honest answer is no, partly or not yet known, that is the answer.
2. Make that sentence the first one after any title. Do not open with the question restated, the method, the history or a chronology.
3. Lead with results and impact, not activity. In an incident or status report, the current state, cause and impact come before the timeline; work done comes after what it achieved, or is left out.
4. Follow with the reasons and evidence the reader needs to trust or act on the answer, most decisive first. Keep any condition that changes the answer in the opening sentence.

Distinct from: `principle-price-each-option` (asking the user to choose), `principle-preserve-meaning` (summarising or re-explaining existing content), `principle-label-the-evidence` (marking each claim's status and source in findings).

Done: the first sentence carries the answer; background, timeline and activity follow it.
