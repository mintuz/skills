# GPS Diagnostic Guide

Use this guide only for the stalled-goal branch. Test each gate in order and stop at the first failure; downstream changes cannot compensate for an upstream break.

## 1. Goal gate

The Goal passes when the user can state one specific, compelling outcome with an observable success measure, a horizon, and protected boundaries.

Ask only what is needed to decide:

- What is the goal in one sentence?
- What observation or number will show that it is achieved?
- Why does it matter personally?
- What must remain protected while pursuing it?

Evidence of a break includes an abstract or frequently changing target, absent success criteria, motivation dominated by obligation, or a timeline and scope the user cannot support.

Repair the Goal by rewriting the outcome and measure, surfacing intrinsic motivation, adding anti-goals, or reducing the scope to a credible milestone.

## 2. Plan gate

The Plan passes when three to five concrete major moves plausibly lead to the Goal, theory and practice confidence are each at least 80%, and the three likeliest failure causes have responses.

Ask only what is needed to decide:

- What are the three to five major moves?
- How confident are you that they will produce the result?
- How confident are you that you will consistently do them?
- Imagine the goal missed at the horizon: what were the three likeliest causes?

Evidence of a break includes too many or vague moves, a missing causal link to the Goal, either confidence score below 80%, dependence on perfect conditions, or unmitigated obstacles.

Repair the Plan by keeping the highest-leverage moves, researching or testing uncertain assumptions, simplifying execution, and pairing each forecasted failure with a response. A two-week test of one move is enough when a full commitment would rest on guesswork.

## 3. System gate

The System passes when progress is visible, recurring moves have reliable cues, and accountability has a named mechanism and cadence.

Ask only what is needed to decide:

- What result and leading actions are being tracked, where, and how often?
- What time, event, or environmental cue starts each recurring move?
- Who or what reviews follow-through, and when?
- What happens after a missed commitment?

Evidence of a break includes stale or absent measurements, forgotten work, unscheduled moves, reliance on motivation, or accountability with no check-in.

Repair the failing mechanism:

- **Tracking:** use one low-friction record, track results and leading actions, and schedule a review.
- **Reminders:** attach each recurring move to a calendar time, existing routine, or visible environmental cue.
- **Accountability:** name a person, group, coach, public commitment, or stakes mechanism with a specific check-in cadence.

## When all gates pass

Inspect the assumptions beneath the map:

- **Execution quality:** the move happened, but not at the intensity or standard the result requires.
- **Timeline:** the expected rate conflicts with relevant evidence or the user's available capacity.
- **Measurement lag:** leading actions are moving while the result measure has not caught up.
- **External constraint:** health, money, access, dependencies, or changed circumstances now limit the route.

Name the weakest supported assumption and define the smallest test that can decide it.

## Recovery situations

| Situation | Targeted response |
|---|---|
| The user wants to give up | Acknowledge the strain, find the first broken gate, simplify the next two weeks, and frame the change as a route adjustment. |
| Execution is inconsistent | Compare successful and missed days, remove the largest friction point, install an exact cue, and add a check-in. |
| Results are slow | Verify the measure, check whether major moves happened consistently and well, reassess the timeline, and evaluate the trend rather than one reading. |
